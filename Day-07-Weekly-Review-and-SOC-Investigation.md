2. Understanding Events, Alerts, and Incidents

I studied the differences between three important cybersecurity terms.

- Event: An observable occurrence in a system or network.
- Alert: A notification generated when activity meets a security detection condition.
- Incident: A security problem requiring an appropriate response.

I learned that an alert does not automatically confirm an attack. Analysts must investigate the available evidence before reaching a conclusion.

3. Suspicious Login Investigation

I analysed a scenario involving repeated failed login attempts followed by a successful login to a Finance account.

I identified the rapid failed attempts as suspicious and suggested examining the source location and the information the account attempted to access.

I also recognised the importance of reviewing authentication logs, checking the circumstances of the successful login, and determining whether the account owner recognises the activity.

This exercise helped me understand how authentication records and related security evidence can support an investigation.

4. Incident Response and Containment

I worked through a scenario in which an unauthorised person was actively using a compromised account to access confidential files.

I selected revoking the active session and restricting the compromised account as the most appropriate immediate response among the options provided.

I learned that containment actions should match the type of compromise. For example, restricting a compromised account is different from isolating a potentially infected computer.

I also reinforced the importance of preserving logs and following established incident-response procedures.

5. Key Takeaways

- An alert requires investigation before conclusions are reached.
- Repeated failed logins followed by a successful login may indicate suspicious activity.
- Authentication logs can help analysts understand login attempts and their outcomes.
- Containment actions should address the specific threat.
- Preserving evidence is important during security investigations.
- Understanding technical concepts must be accompanied by sound decision-making.
