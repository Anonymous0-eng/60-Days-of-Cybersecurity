Day 30 – Networking Fundamentals and Troubleshooting

Date: November 17, 2026
Journey: 60 Days of Cybersecurity

Focus

Networking Fundamentals and Network Troubleshooting.

Objective

To strengthen my understanding of how devices communicate across networks and how networking concepts apply to troubleshooting and cybersecurity.

Activities Completed

Completed a structured lesson covering:

- IP addressing, including private and public IPv4 addresses.
- Default gateways and their role in communication beyond local networks.
- Network Address Translation (NAT) and how private devices communicate with external networks.
- DNS and the process of translating domain names into IP addresses.
- Network troubleshooting commands, including "ipconfig", "ipconfig /all", "ping", "nslookup", and "tracert".
- The relevance of networking knowledge in identifying suspicious connections and investigating network activity.

Key Takeaways

- Connecting to a local network does not necessarily mean internet connectivity is available.
- NAT allows devices with private IP addresses to communicate externally through a public IP address.
- DNS resolution and IP connectivity are separate processes and should be investigated independently.
- Different troubleshooting commands help identify different types of network problems.
- Understanding normal network communication helps cybersecurity analysts recognize unusual activity.

Reflection

Today's session gave me an opportunity to approach networking differently. Instead of relying on question-and-answer exercises, I went through a structured lesson that helped connect concepts I had previously encountered. I gained a clearer understanding of how network components work together and why these concepts matter in cybersecurity.
